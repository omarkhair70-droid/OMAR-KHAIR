## 1. WORK OVERVIEW

**OMAR KHAIR — SELECTED WORKS** is a browser-based exhibition structured as a sequence of encounters rather than a portfolio of projects.

The visitor enters a field that appears to watch back, passes through an uncertain act of contact, meets a responsive digital body, and leaves after that body has disappeared.

The exhibition combines selected source work with material that only came into existence because the exhibition itself demanded it. The browser is treated as the exhibition space, not simply as a container for documentation.

The internal curatorial spine is:

**The room sees you → you attempt contact → contact takes a body → the body disappears → you carry its trace.**

This sentence is an internal structural test, not a required interpretation for the viewer.

---

## 2. ARTIST STATEMENT

I am interested in the moment when an interface stops behaving like a surface and begins to feel like a situation.

In this exhibition, I use the browser as a space for contact, distance, presence and memory. Some parts begin with existing works; others appeared only while making the exhibition. I do not treat that boundary as something the visitor has to solve.

The work moves through being watched, trying to make contact, encountering a body, and what remains when that body is gone.

I am less interested in explaining what each image means than in building the conditions for the encounter and allowing the visitor to leave with their own reading of it.

— **Omar Khair, 2026**

## 3. EXHIBITION SEQUENCE

### WALL OF EYES
**Exhibition-born threshold / procedural browser work**

A field of eyes, broadcast frames and interrupted signals meets the visitor before the first selected work begins.

The room does not wait to be looked at.

### FIRST CONTACT
**Selected work / exhibition encounter**

An object, a distance and a signal are placed between two sides.

Meaning is not guaranteed to cross with the signal. The encounter changes through angle, duration and the visitor's willingness to continue.

### SERAPH / THE BODY
**Selected canonical fragment / interactive digital body**

A responsive body occupies a dark chamber.

Its posture, attention, material state and sound shift through presence, movement, stillness and perception. The encounter is not presented as a model to inspect, but as a presence to remain with.

### FINAL TRACE
**Exhibition-born ending**

The body disappears.

A remembered version of its actual form remains briefly, carrying traces of the encounter before losing its material presence.

Nothing follows it.

---

## 4. WHY THE BROWSER IS THE MEDIUM

The exhibition depends on behaviours that are native to the browser experience:

- pointer / touch presence;
- gaze-like response;
- optional sound;
- haptic response where supported;
- real-time 3D rendering;
- perception and attention state;
- session memory;
- authored transitions between encounters;
- a remembered body whose final state is influenced by the encounter.

The technical system is treated as material only when it contributes to the experience.

---

## 5. INSTALLATION / DISPLAY

Recommended presentation:

- modern desktop or laptop browser;
- fullscreen or distraction-free browser window;
- headphones or speakers available, but sound remains optional;
- mouse / trackpad recommended for desktop interaction;
- stable internet connection for the hosted version;
- begin only from the exhibition entrance: https://omar-khair.vercel.app/

Mobile is supported and documented, but desktop is the preferred presentation for an exhibition or jury setting because the spatial relation to SERAPH is stronger at larger scale.

For a physical exhibition, display on a dedicated monitor with the browser opened at the entrance. Do not place explanatory text over the screen. If wall text is required, use the short exhibition description or artist statement externally.

## 10. CURATORIAL STATUS

**FINAL FINE ART JURY — ACCEPTED / CLOSED**

The exhibition is intentionally short.

No additional project is required after the final trace. The ending is treated as the conclusion of the work rather than an invitation to continue through a larger project list.

Future changes are issue-driven only: bug, accessibility, performance, provenance or a specific documented presentation problem.
