# 3. 100-WORD DESCRIPTION

*OMAR KHAIR — SELECTED WORKS* is a browser-based exhibition structured as an encounter rather than a conventional portfolio. The visitor first enters a procedural field of eyes that appears to watch back, then moves through an act of contact before meeting SERAPH, a responsive real-time digital body. The final state is generated from the same body and carries traces of its last posture, attention and remembered encounter before disappearing. Selected source work sits beside material created specifically by the exhibition. Interaction, sound, 3D space, timing and session memory are treated as artistic material within the browser.

# 4. 200-WORD EXHIBITION DESCRIPTION

*OMAR KHAIR — SELECTED WORKS* is a browser-based exhibition built around gaze, contact, presence and memory.

Rather than presenting projects as a grid or catalogue, the work moves through a single authored sequence. The visitor enters WALL OF EYES, a procedural threshold in which the normal relation between viewer and artwork is reversed: the room appears to be looking first. FIRST CONTACT shifts the experience from observation toward relation through object, distance, angle and signal. SERAPH / THE BODY turns that relation into embodied presence through a responsive real-time 3D figure whose posture, attention, material state and sound react to the encounter.

The exhibition ends by removing the body. Its final state is remembered and re-rendered briefly as a fading trace before the screen settles into near-absence.

Some elements began as independent source works; others only appeared because the exhibition itself required them. That distinction is not presented as a puzzle for the visitor. The browser becomes the exhibition space through its own behaviours: pointer and touch input, optional sound, real-time rendering, temporal transitions and session memory.

The work is intentionally finite. The disappearance of the body is the conclusion, not a transition to another project.

---

# 5. ARTIST STATEMENT

I am interested in the moment when an interface stops behaving like a surface and begins to feel like a situation.

In this exhibition, I use the browser as a space for contact, distance, presence and memory. Some parts begin with existing works; others appeared only while making the exhibition. I do not treat that boundary as something the visitor has to solve.

The work moves through being watched, trying to make contact, encountering a body, and what remains when that body is gone.

I am less interested in explaining what each image means than in building the conditions for the encounter and allowing the visitor to leave with their own reading of it.

— **Omar Khair, 2026**

# 7. APPLICATION ANSWER — WHY THIS MEDIUM?

The browser is not used only as a delivery platform. It gives the work its temporal and relational material: the viewer can be detected through pointer or touch behaviour, sound can be accepted or refused, the 3D body can respond in real time, and the session can carry memory from one encounter into the next. The work would change substantially if reduced to a static image or video.

---

# 8. APPLICATION ANSWER — WHAT IS THE VIEWER'S ROLE?

The viewer is not asked to solve the work or trigger a game objective. Their role shifts throughout the exhibition: first they are watched, then they attempt contact, then they remain with a responsive body. Their interaction leaves a final state that is carried into the ending as a remembered trace before disappearing.

---

# 9. APPLICATION ANSWER — HOW SHOULD IT BE DISPLAYED?

The preferred installation is a dedicated desktop or laptop browser displayed fullscreen or in a distraction-free window, with headphones or speakers available but not required. The visitor begins at the exhibition entrance and moves through the work at their own pace. Explanatory text should sit outside the screen rather than overlay the experience.

---

# 10. APPLICATION ANSWER — RESEARCH / DEVELOPMENT

The exhibition was developed through iterative visual and curatorial research rather than by filling a predetermined project list. The opening WALL OF EYES and final AFTERIMAGE / ABSENCE state were created by the needs of the exhibition itself. Earlier plans containing additional source projects were deliberately reduced when they interrupted the emerging structure. The final work was closed through a Fine Art-style jury pass focused on form, pacing, medium, sound, interpretation and the necessity of each element.
